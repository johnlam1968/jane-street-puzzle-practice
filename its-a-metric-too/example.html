Four steps through the example

  1. 1. The board splits into regions

    Every square belongs to a region, and a region must be symmetric: mirrored or rotated, it still looks the same. This 5×5 splits into four regions, of areas 7, 4, 3 and 11 (third picture).

  2. 2. A capitol is the square every symmetry fixes

    If every symmetry of a region fixes exactly one of its squares, that square is the region's capitol. The dots in the second picture are the three capitols. The region of area 4 has none: its symmetries fix two or four squares, so it can never be the end of a walk.

  3. 3. A number is a cost

    The number in a square is the cheapest total cost of any walk from that square to a capitol. One step inside a region costs that region's area; one step between two regions costs the smaller of the two areas. So the 3 at row 2, column 4 is one step inside the 3-cell region; the 7 at row 4, column 2 is one step inside the 7-cell region; and the 25 at row 5, column 1 is 11 + 7 + 7 — a step inside the 11-cell region, a step across into the 7-cell region, and a step inside it. The two 0s are capitols themselves.

  4. 4. The answer: the sum of the squared row sums

    A row's sum is the total area of the regions that touch that row (a region counts in every row it touches): 37, 29, 31, 43, 55. The answer is the sum of their squares — 37² + 29² + 31² + 43² + 55² = 8045, the official answer.
