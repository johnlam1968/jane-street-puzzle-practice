Play

Paint the states you are assuming, mark the capitols you are assuming — including the hidden ones — and every published number is re-checked against the real travel costs.

Goal

Goal: follow the rules below to cover the whole board with squares

Rules
  1. A state is a connected group of squares that has a mirror or a rotation symmetry of its own.
  2. Its capitol — if every symmetry of the state fixes exactly one of its squares, that square is the capitol; if some symmetry fixes none, or more than one, the state has no capitol. Capitols are never painted: they follow from the shape.
  3. The cost of a step — inside one state a step costs the area of that state; between two states it costs the smaller of the two areas.
  4. The number in a square is the least total cost of any walk from that square to a capitol. A finished tiling has to reproduce every published number and make it the cheapest route there is.
  5. The answer — the row sum of a row is the total area of the states that touch that row (a state counts in every row it touches); the answer is the sum of the squares of the row sums.

The board re-checks every published number against your painting as you go. The badge in the corner of a square says whether that number is settled, still open, asking for a hidden capitol, or contradicted; the legend below the board explains every mark on it.
